# M7 — joint stiffness on hardware, 2026-09-28

**Verdict: the joints are not soft. Every leg joint's servo delivers kp = 45 N·m/rad, as
commanded.** The hypothesis in `HARDWARE_PLAN_2026-09-28.md` §1 (that the real joints are 3–5×
softer than sim) is **refuted**. The 3–5× sim/hardware torque gap is real, but it doesn't come
from stiffness, from `Kt · gear`, or from the URDF link masses. All three were checked here.

For training this means:

- **Do not lower `kp` in sim**, and don't scale effort limits as a stand-in for softness.
- **Hardware torque in the reports is real commanded torque.** The "hardware torque" and
  saturation figures in the reports are reconstructed as `kp·err − kd·vel`. That reconstruction
  turned out to be exactly what the ESC commands: at rest, ESC-reported torque equals
  `kp · error` on every joint (per-joint medians 44.8–45.2, every point within 3%). So the
  saturation fractions (23–34% of ticks on hips, knees and ankles) are real demand the ESC clips, not an artifact of the
  reconstruction.
- **The gap is in something this static test cannot see.** The hardware position error really is
  3–5× larger than sim's. With stiffness, torque constant and mass ruled out, the candidates are
  dynamic or unmodelled resistance (listed in "What is still open" below).

## Method

- The robot hung free, feet off the ground. All 12 leg joints were held in POSITION throughout;
  each pass swept one joint type on both legs. The limb's own weight is the load: URDF link masses
  sum to **12.612 kg**, against **12.61 kg** measured (0.015%).
- Gravity torque was computed from the **measured** pose of all 12 joints, so a held joint that
  sagged is still accounted for. `position_ki` was read back from every ESC and confirmed **0**
  before moving, so the steady-state error is a direct read of stiffness.
- **Two independent readings per point:**
  1. **ESC torque / position error.** This is the stiffness the servo actually applies. ESC torque
     is `Kt · gear · I` from measured current, verified against `Kt · gear` within 0.5% on all
     joints.
  2. **ESC torque / URDF gravity torque.** This checks `Kt · gear · efficiency` and the mass model
     together, against physics.
- **Schedule:** 8 passes, 6 load steps each, stopping at a torque peak inside the range or 3° short
  of a hardstop.
  - `hip_pitch` and `knee_pitch` swept both legs in the same direction.
  - `hip_roll` and `hip_yaw` swept the two legs in opposite device directions (the same direction
    in the world) so they don't collide, then swapped.
- Tool: `scripts/measure/m7_sweep.py`. Data: `m7_sweep_20260928T212236.json` (pass 1) and
  `m7_sweep_20260928T212419.json` (passes 2–8).

### What the residuals say

- **Hips and right knee: the model is right.** Gravity and ESC torque agree within about ±7%, and
  the extra torque scatters around zero at ±0.5 N·m. That scatter is static friction. It holds a
  different share of the load at each settle.
- **Ankle pitch has about 0.4 N·m of friction on both sides**, present even near zero gravity load.
  Hanging, that is as large as the entire gravity load on the joint; it's about 6% of the 7 N·m
  cap. The actuator model should include it.
- **Left knee only: extra torque grows with angle** (+0.2 → +0.9 N·m at 94°). The right knee shows
  none. That points to something specific to the left leg: a cable, a rubbing part, or mass the
  URDF doesn't have. Worth knowing when reading L/R gait asymmetry, but at under 1 N·m against a
  12 N·m cap it doesn't explain 25–30% saturation.

### Numbers the tool prints that should NOT be used

`m7_sweep.py` also fits a gravity-only stiffness per joint, and from it derives L/R ratios,
direction asymmetry and a "frame" column. **Ignore these.**
- **Stiffness fit.** It runs through 3–6 points and is dominated by the ±0.5 N·m friction offsets
  above, which is why it ranges from 10 to 62. The "left ankle 2.16× stiffer" line is this
  artifact.
- **Direction asymmetry.** It can't be separated from friction by sweeps that only move one way.
- **Frame column.** The sign test is blind when the torque curve is nearly symmetric. It labelled
  `left_hip_yaw` "flipped" even though that joint's limits match the URDF exactly.

The servo-kp column above is the stiffness measurement.

### Contact event (excluded)

Pass 5 turned both hip_yaw joints the same world direction to ±53°. The right foot hit the left
foot at right +38° (the operator saw it). The ESC then delivered 8.3 N·m beyond gravity, and the
contact guard aborted the pass. Both legs' points from the first contact step onward are excluded.
The partner joint's torque was contaminated too: left hip_yaw needed *less* torque as its load
rose. **hip_yaw pairs are now capped at ±32°.** For training this means the feet meet when both
legs yaw the same world direction beyond roughly 34–38°.

## What is still open (not measured by a static test)

This test was static. The gap has to be in something only motion reveals. Ranked by how cheaply
each can be tested on this rig:

1. **Response time.** The firmware applies `torque_filter_alpha = 0.1454`, about a 50 Hz
   first-order filter on the 2 kHz position loop. The sim actuator has no equivalent. A step
   response on the hanging leg measures it directly; this is M7 item 4.
2. **Reflected rotor inertia (armature) and damping.** Not measured. They are unobservable at rest.
3. **Friction under motion.** Static friction is measured above (ankles 0.4 N·m, others under
   0.3 N·m median). Friction while moving isn't.
4. **Saturation point.** Delivered torque versus demand above the cap. Needs a known load larger
   than gravity provides here (M7 item 3).
5. **Transport delay.** Already measured: uniform 0–10 ms, all joints
   (`TRAINING_INPUT.json` → `transport`).

**What this rules out for training:** softening `kp`, correcting `Kt`/gear, and changing link masses.
Each is verified. The static ankle friction (~0.4 N·m) is the one new actuator parameter measured
here.
